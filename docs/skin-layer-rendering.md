# 3D Skin Layers rendering

The reference is [tr7zw/3d-Skin-Layers at 80f19a0](https://github.com/tr7zw/3d-Skin-Layers/tree/80f19a0cdc3091726f2885eb60c12ba2b7a4700c), using its default player settings. The implementation follows `SolidPixelWrapper`, `CustomizableCube`, `SkinUtil`, and `OffsetProvider`. No upstream Java implementation is shipped in the application.

Each nonzero-alpha overlay pixel has a full voxel. Neighbor visibility distinguishes opaque and translucent pixels, wraps across cuboid edges, omits buried backs and trims shared corner walls into triangles. The starting corner and full pixel UV rectangle match the mod, including trimmed faces. Rendering uses Minecraft's explicit `0,1,2,2,3,0` quad triangulation, with winding reversed for editor culling, and the original direction/pixel/face submission order. This matters for overlapping translucent samples under MSAA. Default scales are 1.15 for pixels, 1.05 for torso width and 1.18 for the head. Limb height is 1.035, with the mod's arm and head pivot corrections. Fast rendering batches the exterior into a textured cuboid while retaining the same side geometry and picking coordinates.

**Options > 3D Skin Layers settings** exposes three scales, six layer toggles, 3D render distance (5–40 blocks), render resolution (50–200%), and antialiasing (Off/2x/4x/8x MSAA). Reset restores layer and quality defaults; Apply saves them, and Cancel leaves settings unchanged. Lighting is managed exclusively in the main Minecraft Lighting controls, and this dialog preserves it even after Reset. Fast face batching is an internal optimization, not a quality control. A disabled layer or a player beyond the distance threshold uses the ordinary flat layer. Standalone skull scales and Minecraft-specific Iris/Sodium compatibility switches are not exposed.

## Colors and image quality

The visual target is **vanilla Minecraft at 100% in-game brightness**. Minecraft lighting applies independently of 3D Skin Layers: the regular base skin, flat overlays, raised layers, first-person hands and combined accessories share the same lighting when enabled. No extra editor fill light is added. The reference is the [official Minecraft client](https://piston-data.mojang.com/v1/objects/e877b6a07acd633fb3bb475002175cec036e7b87/client.jar), SHA-1 `e877b6a07acd633fb3bb475002175cec036e7b87`, specifically `Lightmap`, `LightmapRenderStateExtractor`, `Lighting`, `assets/minecraft/shaders/core/lightmap.fsh`, `assets/minecraft/shaders/include/light.glsl`, the Overworld dimension attributes and the day timeline. The light directions, diffuse expression and daylight calculation were also checked against the supplied Minecraft 26.3 source. Non-player/custom models retain their existing unlit rendering.

**Brightness uses world-light levels 0-15**, with a single-number readout and a default of **8**, the nearest step to the midpoint. The short Brightness slider, longer Lighting angle slider and Elytra controls live beside Fill/Brush settings in the tool-options area. Brightness and Lighting angle stack vertically as two compact rows, with labels beside their sliders so they fit within the normal tool-options height. They wrap in narrow windows, remain available across tool switches, and follow the tool-area collapse button. Both hide in 2D without losing their values. Brightness requires Minecraft lighting; elytra spread requires combined elytra mode. The **Minecraft Lighting** toolbar button toggles lighting, retaining the light level, angle and surroundings preference and hiding its controls when lighting is off (first person keeps the disabled controls in place to preserve the hand's screen position). The button appears in every 3D or hybrid view, including when 3D Skin Layers is off. Switching layer modes preserves the lighting preference and brightness. The layer-settings Reset leaves all lighting preferences unchanged.

**Surroundings**, checked by default beside Brightness, applies the same world-light factor to the 3D background and grass ground. Uncheck it to keep the surroundings fully bright while the skin remains lit. This is a simple brightness adjustment for the preview environment; it does not simulate a Minecraft sky or weather. It works in regular, combined and first-person previews, including scaled/MSAA rendering. The 2D texture sheet, orientation cube and editor controls retain their colors.

The mod's [`CustomizableModelPart`](https://github.com/tr7zw/3d-Skin-Layers/blob/80f19a0cdc3091726f2885eb60c12ba2b7a4700c/src/main/java/dev/tr7zw/skinlayers/render/CustomizableModelPart.java) passes Minecraft's packed world light through to its vertices. Minecraft has separate sky and block light channels. The main slider selects **sky light**, under **Overworld noon**, with **block light 0**, and no weather, night vision, darkness or boss effects. This is a reproducible vanilla lighting setup, not a reconstruction of any world screenshot. Torch light, time-of-day colors, fog and shader packs are outside this preview's scope.

The Minecraft Overworld has an ambient light color of **#0a0a0a**. This is added even when both world-light channels are zero. For neutral daylight, the shader normalizes the level by 15, applies `n / (4 - 3*n)`, adds `10/255` ambient light, clamps to 0-1, and blends toward `1 - (1-c)^4` using the game's brightness/gamma setting. It then stores the result in an RGBA8 lightmap.

At 100% game brightness, the calculated 16 grayscale lightmap texels are `38, 53, 69, 86, 104, 122, 141, 160, 179, 198, 215, 231, 244, 252, 255, 255`. Level 0 therefore retains ambient light; it does not multiply the skin by zero. Low light still looks dark, as it does in an unlit vanilla location. CPU/GPU floating-point and UNORM conversion can differ by one channel step at quantization boundaries.

World rendering defaults to Minecraft's normalized directions `(0.2, 1, -0.7)` and `(-0.2, 1, 0.7)`. The compact **Lighting angle** control below Brightness rotates both directions around the vertical axis from **-90° to 90°**, preserving their elevation. The two equal lights swap places after 180°, so the endpoints render identically; both remain selectable. Older saved angles normalize to their visually equivalent value in this range. Drag its standard slider, type a signed integer in the degree field, or use the numeric arrows. Its **Default** button restores **0°**, using the exact original directions and leaving brightness and quality settings unchanged. A nonzero angle is a custom preview adjustment. The angle is saved and survives lighting/layer/view switches and the layer-settings Reset. The controls follow Brightness visibility, including remaining disabled in place when first-person lighting is off.

The main toolbar shows **Minecraft Lighting** first, as a square grass block with light rays, followed by the **3D Skin Layers** icon labeled **3D**, with localized tooltips and accessible names. Both icons stay on the main toolbar when longer names such as Steve/Alex (Minimal) are selected.

The editor converts the vertical axis and transforms normals with the current mesh pose, using the vanilla diffuse expression `min(1, 0.4 + 0.6 * (max(0, dot(light0, normal)) + max(0, dot(light1, normal))))`. Rotating the camera does not rotate the world lights. Diffuse shading is multiplied by the lightmap, while alpha and source texture pixels remain unchanged. Angle changes refresh cached vertex colors without rebuilding skin geometry.

For texture RGB `(211,235,234)`, a front face at level 0 renders approximately `(23,26,26)` and at full daylight approximately `(156,174,173)`. Game brightness/gamma is fixed at 100%; only world-light level is adjustable. Turning off Minecraft lighting provides an unlit texture-color preview and fully bright surroundings. Old `PreviewBrightness`, `PreviewGameBrightness` and `PreviewLightDirection` preferences are ignored.

Resolution defaults to 100% and antialiasing to Off. Higher resolution supersamples the 3D scene before downsampling; lower resolution reduces GPU work. MSAA is resolved at the render resolution before scaling to the window. Both settings apply live, including through the existing antialiasing menu, and affect only the 3D view. Skin textures use nearest-neighbor sampling; 2D editing, picking, PNG contents and the UI remain at native resolution. Supported sample counts and framebuffer dimensions are bounded by the graphics driver. Framebuffers are reused between frames and released when returning to native resolution without AA or closing the editor.

## Validation

Preview metadata is cached by texture identity, upload version and dimensions. Unchanged frames reuse textures, alpha masks, transparency classifications and layer geometry. Hover previews pass their prepared CPU pixels directly to the layer cache, avoiding a second GPU readback. Alpha edits invalidate affected geometry and transparency; RGB-only edits retain geometry. Settings changes, reloads, resizing and document switches refresh the appropriate caches.

The smoke suite covers Steve, Alex, legacy hats and higher-resolution textures, corner geometry, translucent pixels, UVs, picking, mesh caching, settings persistence and reset. These checks use synthetic textures and require no upstream source or reference downloads.

Graphics checks cover regular base skins, flat overlays and raised faces, all 16 light levels, adjustable angles and exact Default restoration, both rendering backends, unlit colors, the lighting toolbar toggle with layers on and off, live resolution/MSAA changes, framebuffer reuse and cleanup, painting and undo, and unchanged 2D views. Run them with:

```powershell
dotnet run --project tests/MCSkin3D.SmokeTests -c Release
dotnet run --project tests/MCSkin3D.IntegrationTests -c Release -- --render-quality
```
